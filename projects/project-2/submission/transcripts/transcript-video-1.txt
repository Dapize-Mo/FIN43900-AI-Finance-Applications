[00:25 - 01:15] HOW MY THINKING CHANGED SINCE LAB 13
"When we first opened Lab 13 and looked at the Meridian Foundation Reserve case, my instinct—like many analysts—was simply to chase the highest Sharpe ratio or take the client's 60/40 policy benchmark as an acceptable starting point.
However, building the algorithmic mandate checker forced a fundamental shift in my thinking. When we codified Fact S3—the non-negotiable legal requirement to maintain 12 months of quarterly spending payouts, or $3.0 million, in daily-liquid sovereign paper—we uncovered that the Foundation's own 60/40 benchmark fails the mandate. Holding 40% in corporate bonds leaves zero percent in sovereign Treasuries, which creates an immediate covenant breach.
I realized that fiduciary portfolio management is not an unconstrained mathematical search for return; it is a constraint-satisfaction problem where legal liquidity, solvency, and drawdowns strictly govern the feasible solution set."

[01:15 - 02:15] THE AI OUTPUT I INDEPENDENTLY CHECKED
"A critical requirement of our model risk framework under Federal Reserve SR 26-2 is independent challenge. When generating our multi-asset covariance matrix and grid search optimizer, I prompted Claude to generate the portfolio variance calculation and covariance builder.
The model provided a standard nested loop, but did not automatically validate whether the input correlation matrix was symmetric or whether diagonal elements were bounded to exactly 1.0. If an AI generates a covariance matrix where correlation between equities and bonds is asymmetric, the resulting quadratic surface can produce non-convex artifacts and false global minima.
I independently intervened, wrote a mathematical input validation contract in `mandate.py` that verifies symmetry within ten to the minus ninth tolerance, confirmed that every diagonal entry equals exactly 1.0, and checked the resulting portfolio volatility against our hand-calculated Lab 14 known answers. Only after the code reconciled to the exact basis point did I permit its admission into the project engine."
