[01:20 - 02:25] THE MANDATE AND RISK ENGINE
"In `mandate.py`, we translate the Meridian Foundation's contractual covenants into hard algorithmic rules:
Lines 85 to 135 implement `check_mandate()`. It evaluates six distinct criteria: total allocation summing to 100%, long-only non-negative weights, a minimum 3.0% reserve-eligible sovereign liquidity floor, a 6.50% net return hurdle (reflecting CPI of 2.5% plus the 4.0% real payout hurdle), a 90% five-day liquidity requirement, and an 8% single-issuer limit for non-exempt assets.
If we inspect `engine.py`, lines 25 to 70 construct the covariance matrix Sigma from sleeve volatilities and correlation coefficients.
Lines 110 to 160 implement `optimize_constrained_mix()`. Rather than using an unconstrained solver that might output extreme or leveraged weights, we execute a discrete 2% step grid search that evaluates every feasible long-only asset combination, instantly filtering out any allocation that breaches the 3.0% sovereign buffer or fails the 6.50% net return hurdle, and selecting the portfolio with the global minimum annualized volatility."

[02:25 - 03:30] POINT-IN-TIME DATA & THE PROVENANCE GATE
"Now let's examine `point_in_time.py`. In quantitative finance, naive backtesting routinely creates look-ahead contamination.
Our module enforces a dual-timestamp coordinate system: Event Date, when economic activity occurred, and Publication Date, when the BLS actually released the data.
In Lab 17, we proved that on September 30, 2026, the September CPI print was unknowable—it was not published until mid-October. Even more critically, comparing ALFRED's vintage of January 15, 2024 to today proves that 60 prior monthly observations were revised due to annual seasonal factor recalculations.
Our provenance gate places CPI under strict Fiduciary Quarantine: we only admit observations published prior to or on the decision date."

[03:30 - 04:30] TESTS & VALIDATION AUDIT
