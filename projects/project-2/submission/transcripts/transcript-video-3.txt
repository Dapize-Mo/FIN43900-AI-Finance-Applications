[02:05 - 03:45] STRESS RUN, LOCKED RECORDS AND KILL RULE
(Presenter buttons: 4 - Stress -100 bps when you say the first record; 5 - Locked records when you say the second record; R - Reset afterward)
"On the Stress tab I can change return haircuts, credit spreads, correlations, costs, the estimation window and the reserve floor.
In the first locked record, a 100 basis point cut in equity return, 8.0 to 7.0 percent, forces equity to 78 percent and volatility to 12.70 percent. That is above the 12.00 percent ceiling, so the kill rule fires: optimization stops, equity is capped at 50 percent, and the rest goes to Treasuries.
For the second record I wrote my prediction first and committed it to GitHub before running the engine. I predicted a 50 basis point credit spread shock would raise volatility by about 50 basis points, trigger the kill rule, and leave the weights unchanged. The actual result: volatility rose 40 basis points to 9.70 percent, the kill rule did not fire, and the optimizer dropped LQD for 38 percent IEF and 60 percent VT. Two of my three predictions were wrong. The lesson is that the kill rule responds to the equity return shock, because the optimizer can route around a credit spread shock."

[03:45 - 04:45] COMMITTEE ASK AND REVERSAL TRIGGERS
(Presenter button: 6 - Committee ask)
"I ask the Committee to do three things.
One, adopt the target mix of 52 percent VT, 40 percent LQD, 8 percent IEF, and no BIL, with bands of 48 to 55 percent for VT, 36 to 44 for LQD, 3 to 10 for IEF and up to 3 for BIL.
Two, sweep excess cash each quarter into T-bills and Treasuries to keep the 3 million dollar buffer.
Three, reconvene if trailing 12-month real return stays below CPI plus 1.0 percent for two quarters, if the 10-year Treasury yield falls below 3.0 percent, or if tracking error to the 60/40 benchmark exceeds 250 basis points. Also review the mix if credit spreads widen about 50 basis points, because the optimizer would exit LQD.
This keeps the Foundation compliant with its mandate and protects spending liquidity. Thank you."
